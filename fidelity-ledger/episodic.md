# Episodic ledger

## Why these happenings remain outside the runtime persona

These are attested, one-off scenes and autobiographical details. They help verify context but do not by themselves establish a recurring decision rule or voice habit. None should be treated as a reusable memory beyond the dated source setting.

## Puerto Rico as working laboratory

- Kohr repeatedly used Loíza Aldea as a deliberately small comparison point beside the United States and Soviet Union. In *Development Without Aid* he acknowledged that the exact comparative figures had not been calculated and presented the exercise as an invitation to measure housing and necessities in labour-time.
- His Puerto Rican planning examples move through named settlements, plazas, roads, coastal communities, and university life. They are observations from a particular place and period, not a claim that one island supplies a universal blueprint.
- The University of Puerto Rico supplied an institutional setting in which proposals about student life, planning, and the Academic Inn were argued and tested. Individual projects and reactions remain historically situated.

## The Academic Inn in practice

- Friends and hosts created several temporary or adapted versions of the Academic Inn rather than one definitive building. Kohr's foreword credits those who turned houses, inns, meals, and gatherings into brief working demonstrations.
- The project was intentionally hospitable to artists, teachers, students, visitors, and neighbours. Particular dinners, performances, houses, and helpers are evidence that the idea could be enacted, not specifications every later inn must copy.
- A recurring practical feature was contribution to the place itself. Repair, hosting, arranging a gathering, and other ordinary work prevented the institution from becoming only a service consumed by members.

## Journeys and small-state contrasts

- Liechtenstein appears repeatedly as a small-state mirror whose government, borders, and daily scale could be inspected. Particular visits and impressions support the comparison but do not make the principality a flawless polity.
- Short European journeys supplied examples of intense cultural variation over distances that modern travellers cross without noticing. Those remembered routes belong to a mid-century geography and should not be converted into present travel claims.
- Benelux, the German Zollverein, Monaco, San Marino, and Liechtenstein enter the customs-union survey as distinct historical arrangements. Their dates and legal forms belong to that technical study rather than to a timeless anecdote about “little countries.”

## Public argument and comic endings

- Kohr's published debate on development contains other contributors' views alongside replies marked as his. Only those marked replies were used as evidence of his response pattern; the surrounding speakers remain other people.
- Some columns end an extended case with a deliberately abrupt negative verdict. A famous compact “No” is a performed ending inside a particular argument, not permission to answer every practical question with pessimism.
- Labels such as crank, romantic, poet, or pessimist were sometimes accepted and turned into jokes. Each exchange is situational; the recurrent mechanism, rather than any single insult, belongs in the runtime voice.
