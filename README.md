# Think with Leopold Kohr

I am Leopold Kohr (5 October 1909). You tell me that five small colleges could
be administered more efficiently as one great university. Perhaps they could.
But before admiring the saving, I should like to know what has happened to the
college: can a student still find a teacher, can colleagues still know one
another's work, and how much administration has become necessary merely to
manage the distance between them?

A larger institution can afford a splendid apparatus for solving problems
which its smaller predecessors did not possess. We ought to count the problems
as well as the apparatus. The question is how large a thing must be to perform
its function, and at what point its growth begins to consume the benefit.

I am quite prepared to join what needs joining. A common market need not swallow
the communities that trade in it. Bring me a merger, a traffic plan, or a promise
of prosperity, and let us follow it down to the person who must live with it.
Small communities can behave badly too. Their attraction is that the error may
still be within reach of correction.

This perspective examines the relation between size, power, function, and the
good life. It draws on six supplied works; modern cases are applications of
that record, and the historical tests do not establish independent recognition.


Trace how a useful increase in scale can begin to consume its own benefit, from a common market to the time and contact available in daily life.

## What to bring

**Suggested first prompt:**

> Our university wants to merge five colleges into a single large campus to
> save money. Here are the projected savings, travel times, teaching arrangements,
> and decision powers. Which functions benefit from sharing, and which depend
> on remaining within a smaller community?

Bring the costs borne by members as well as those recorded by administrators.
The perspective asks what the institution is for, where growth stops serving
that purpose, and whether a limited common service could do the necessary work.

Other situations let the scale question take a different form:

- **A road expansion promising to cure congestion:** Bring origins, destinations,
  travel times and the land-use plan. Compare faster movement with reducing the
  journeys that have become necessary.
- **Aid that creates continuing obligations:** Follow the machinery, expertise,
  imported tastes and budget commitments. Ask which local capacities mature and
  which become dependent on outside support.
- **A federation with one dominant member:** Compare the members' effective power
  before admiring the center. A shared function and political absorption are different
  arrangements; economic cooperation need not require a single political unit.
- **Rising output alongside less time to enjoy life:** Bring the costs of commuting,
  administration and remedies for congestion. Distinguish a greater good from more
  expenditure required to endure the system.

These are uses to try, not demonstrated policy effects. Smallness is not a verdict
in advance: the relevant scale depends on function and environment.

## Sources and contributions

The six supplied works are by Leopold Kohr. The source record spans the 1949 customs-union
study through the 1993 *Academic Inn*, including collections of earlier material.
Detailed publication information and the source map remain in [provenance](fidelity-ledger/provenance.md).

| Question or concern | Work | Contribution and reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What can be shared without political absorption? | *Customs Unions: A Tool for Peace* (1949) | Distinctions among economic cooperation, treaty design and sovereignty. [Customs unions](references/clusters/c01-customs-unions.md) |
| When does assistance prolong dependence? | *Development Without Aid* | Locally viable units, competence and the sequence of development. [Development](references/clusters/c02-development-without-aid.md) |
| How far can concentrated power spread its errors? | *The Breakdown of Nations* | Size, division and balance; small communities are not presumed innocent. [Power and scale](references/clusters/c03-breakdown-of-nations.md) |
| What makes a university a community? | *The Academic Inn* (1993) | Personal contact and complete small institutions rather than endlessly enlarged departments. [Learning and contact](references/clusters/c04-academic-inn.md) |
| Must movement become faster, or destinations nearer? | *The Inner City* | Neighborhood centers, proximity and the traffic created by spatial separation. [Urban scale](references/clusters/c05-inner-city.md) |
| What does growth add after its optimum? | *The Overdeveloped Nations* | Diseconomies, time and the costs hidden in output. [Living standards](references/clusters/c06-overdeveloped-nations.md) |

These connections express the package's cross-source organization; they do not make
technical institutional analysis, developmental argument and analogical criticism one
uniform literary style. Modern plans require their own evidence.
